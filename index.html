    Sag of the outer tube (estimate)

    Clamped–clamped beam, fused silica (E = 72 GPa, ρ = 2200 kg/m³). The outer tube carries the weight of the tubes lying inside it. Femtosecond focus depth is a few tens of µm, so the head also follows the measured height map.

    A lathe-style layout on a granite bed. The tube assembly turns on a horizontal axis between two synchronized rotary axes. The laser stays fixed and its beam is relayed by mirrors to a light focusing head that travels along the tube underneath it.

    Includes one height-map scan per seam at 50 mm/s and manual loading times (90 s load, 45 s unload per part; 4 min to fill the jacket). The animation itself is compressed.

    Primary pitch radius is 18.5 mm, not 20.5 mm

    A primary touching the jacket wall has its centre at jacket ID/2 − primary OD/2 = 28.5 − 10 = 18.5 mm. At the 20.5 mm in the drawing it would cut 2 mm into the jacket wall. At 18.5 mm neighbouring primaries are 1.75 mm apart and the open core is Ø17 mm.

    θ = 60° needs smaller primaries

    Six tubes at 60° fit in a 57 mm bore only up to OD 19.0 mm. With 20 mm primaries they overlap by 1.5 mm. The Preform tab recalculates this for any θ.

    The laser box stays still

    The S-50-1030-F2 is a single 55 kg box. Moving it 1.1 m per seam would load the axis and its water lines for no gain. It sits on a stand behind the bed, below the granite top, and a periscope lifts the beam. Only two mirrors, a Z stage and the objective travel. The 3× expander keeps the beam size steady over the 1.1 m change in path length.

    Weld from below, at 6 o'clock

    An inner tube lies on the bottom of the tube around it, so gravity keeps the contact line closed along the full length. Fused silica is transparent at 1030 nm, and the femtosecond pulses only melt glass at the focus on the interface, so the beam comes up through the outer wall. Holding a tertiary against a top wall instead would fight a sag of about 1.3 mm between clamped ends.

    Drive both ends

    A1 and A2 index together under electronic gearing. If only one end turned, the 1.2 m assembly would twist each time it indexed.

    Index accuracy

    Placing a seam within ±10 µm on the 28.5 mm jacket wall needs ±0.020° (±72″) on the A axis. A harmonic-drive axis with a direct encoder can hold that.

    Assumptions

    One tertiary per secondary, one secondary per primary, N primaries in the jacket. 60 mm stays unwelded at each end for the chuck jaws and end plates. The weld speed, laser settings and handling times are placeholders until weld trials. Machine dimensions are a starting layout for the engineering drawing.

    Open items for the drawing stage

    Objective NA and working distance, and focus correction through the curved 1.5 mm wall. The weld window from trials (pulse energy, burst, repetition rate, speed). Manual loading or a loader. Jaw material, enclosure layout and interlocks for Class 4.
